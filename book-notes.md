## 12. Soreness rule

## 5. Flexibility standard (full, supersedes partial above)
Book says: Chapter 2 gives FIVE flexibility tests, each graded Poor/Fair/Good/Very Good/Excellent (p.15 list): Table, Jackknife, Bow, Corkscrew, Jerzy Squat. The Overhead Squat was rejected as the flexibility test; the better test is a modification called the Candle Squat / "Jerzy Squat" (Fig 2.5, 2.10).
Jerzy Squat (flexibility of most joints): stand straight, feet together, toes curled up; lift arms above head holding a book on open palms; keeping heels on the ground and arms vertical with elbows locked, squat as far as you can without leaning forward. Poor: barely bend knees. Fair: thighs higher than parallel. Good: thighs parallel. Very Good: thighs lower than parallel. Excellent: sit on your calves, totally relaxed.
Table (p.16-17): feet below hips, toes curled up, knees locked, back arched, bend forward, fingertips: Poor cannot reach knees; Fair reach knees; Good mid-shin; Very Good reach floor; Excellent palms flat on floor.
Jackknife (p.17): sit, legs straight, toes curled up toward you, bend forward: Poor hands directly over knees; Fair touch toes; Good palms rest on bottom of feet; Very Good palms touch floor in front of feet; Excellent elbows reach toes. (Owner's pencil note questions "not arched?" for Jackknife — book's text says only "Bend forward"; figure 2.2 says rounded back.)
Bow (p.18): lie on stomach facing a wall, palms up, arch spine, eyes upward: Poor look straight down at floor; Fair bottom of wall; Good middle of wall; Very Good edge of ceiling; Excellent straight up at ceiling.
Corkscrew (p.19): stand in middle of square room facing wall, feet under hips, stick behind neck, elbows down, forearms perpendicular to floor; twist right: Poor wall to your right (90°); Fair second corner right (135°); Good wall behind you (180°); Very Good third corner right (225°); Excellent wall to your left (270°). Repeat left.

## 2. Rest between rounds (book, p.69 Ch.5 "Recovery and Relaxation During Exercise")
Book says: Rest is built into EVERY REPETITION, not between rounds. Each repetition has three parts: 1. inhale, 2. move while holding the breath, 3. exhale — the exhale is when you recover and relax (mental release, "micro-meditation"). The program "is a form of interval training... provides for rest between repetitions" (vs. endurance programs with continuous training). Each exhalation lets the brain restore hormones that stimulate muscle fibers. No rest interval between sets/rounds/sequences is prescribed on this page.
